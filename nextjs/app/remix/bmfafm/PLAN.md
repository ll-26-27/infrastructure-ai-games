# The Race on the world stage: the plan

This game keeps The Race at its centre (from the BMFAFM game, at `/race` and `/race/rules`) and folds in ideas from the other groups' games. It lives at `/remix/bmfafm`.

## What stays from The Race

Everything that makes The Race recognisable stays, with its numbers: the board of three rings around Superintelligence, the gates and the sprint inward, data centers, incidents and summits, the five players (United States, China, US Lab A, US Lab B, Chinese Lab) and their powers, each player's bag of white and black tokens, the hidden-compute envelopes and inspections, the doom track and the AI pawn, the Incident deck, and the pause treaty with its shared win. The Race's argument stays too: a pause pays most to whoever breaks it, and whoever wins the race may still lose everyone.

## From the CNA game (branch cna-game)

The CNA game is a strategy game about AI proliferation and AI safety: countries choose cards in secret each year, then reveal them together, while two world meters, risk and tension, rise for everyone.

| What we take | Where it goes in The Race | What it changes |
|---|---|---|
| **World tension**, the CNA game's second world meter | A tension track beside the doom track, also drawn as a red ring around the board that glows as tension rises. Covert ops push it up; it eases by 1 at the end of every round, as tension does every year in the CNA game; signing a treaty and the peace-summit wildcard bring it down further. At 10: world war. | The Race already has one way for everyone to lose to the machine (the doom track). Now there is a second, human one: everyone can lose to each other. Racing is no longer the only danger; fighting over the race is one too. |
| **Attack cards**, played on another country | **Covert ops**, one per turn, on the player you target: Hack, Send spies, Honey trap a lab's CEO (only against a lab), Military strike (only against a player who owns a data center). Each costs capital and is decided by a die roll (4 or more works). A successful hack or strike costs the target their next turn, as a successful attack does in the CNA game. | The Race's players could only slow each other with their powers and inspections. Now they can sabotage, but every op raises world tension, so the move that helps you win the race pushes everyone toward war. A spy in place makes your next op likelier, the way spies do in the CNA game. |
| **Political systems**: the same card does different things in a democracy and an autocracy | The United States and its labs are democracies; China and the Chinese Lab are autocracies. An autocracy's ops roll 1 higher, and no one can inspect its envelope unless a treaty is in force. A democracy's failed ops cost 2 more capital (a free press finds out), and it inspects for 1 capital, not 2. | The Race's sides become different kinds of player, not just different starting numbers. The game argues what Winner argues on his idea card: technical choices have politics built in. |
| **Wildcards**: choose one of three real events from the world database | Each player is dealt three wildcards at the start and three more each time they step inward; playing one is an action. The events are the CNA game's, rescaled to The Race's compute and capital: a chip shortage, export controls, an earthquake in Taiwan, a chips subsidy, a model's weights leak, a peace summit, a drought, an outage, and a safety incident with a killswitch. | Real infrastructure breaks into the game: water, chips, grids, leaks. Stepping inward now also gives you new ways to change the world, so the sprint is tempting for a second reason. |
| **Simultaneous reveal**: every card is chosen in secret and revealed at once | The summit's treaty vote is now secret. The screen passes to each player in turn, everyone votes yes or no in private, and the votes are revealed together. | In The Race, everyone voted in the open, so no one could say yes and hope the others said no. Now a player can vote no without being seen to break ranks until the reveal. It is harder to know who wants the pause. |
| **Multiple Loss Conditions** | Three ways for everyone to lose: the AI takes over (doom 12), an unsafe superintelligence (a black draw at the center), and world war (tension 10). | More of the game is about keeping the world alive long enough for anyone to win. |

What we leave out of the CNA game, for now: its world map, its five bars per country (AI progress, safety, cybersecurity, allyship, economy) and its ten-year clock. The Race already measures progress by position on the board and safety by the bag, so two sets of bars would say the same thing twice.

## From the break-the-datacentre game (branch break-the-datacentre)

This game has not arrived yet. Once it does, we will read it and fold in what fits. The place for it is already in The Race: **data centers are spaces on the board that players buy, earn compute from, and charge rent on**, so a game about breaking a data centre has somewhere to land. Depending on what the game does, it could become:

- a new covert op or a set of ops aimed at a rival's data centers (cutting power, water or cooling), alongside the strike;
- data centers with weak points (power, water, cooling) that incidents, wildcards or attacks can knock out for a while instead of destroying;
- a defence a player can buy for a data center, so that protecting infrastructure becomes a choice that costs compute you could have spent racing.

We will credit each piece to the break-the-datacentre game on the game's page.

## Where the ideas come from in the kit

- **Mechanics** from the mechanics list: from The Race, Press Your Luck, Race, Negotiation, Secret Traitor and Variable Player Powers; from the CNA game, Simultaneous Action Selection, Voting, Take That, Wild Card and Multiple Loss Conditions.
- **Idea cards:** Bostrom and Bogost (from The Race); Perrow on tightly coupled systems and Winner on the politics of technical choices (from the CNA game).
- **Records** in the world database: a hack, a model's weights leak, a chip shortage, export controls, an earthquake in Taiwan, the CHIPS Act, a drought, an outage, a safety incident, Taiwan, and the governments of the United States and China.
