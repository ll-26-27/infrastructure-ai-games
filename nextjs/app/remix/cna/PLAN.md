# The CNA game, remixed: the plan

This combined game keeps **the CNA game** (branch `cna-game`, route `/cna-game`) at its centre and folds in ideas from the other groups' games. You still play the president of a country in the AI race, on the same world map, with the same five bars (AI progress, AI safety, cybersecurity, allyship, economy), the same two world meters (AI risk and tension), the same decade (2027 to 2036), and the same secret cards revealed all at once. What changes is what happens around the cards.

## From the BMFAFM game (*The Race*, `/race` and `/race/rules`)

### 1. The bag of safe and doom tokens

- **What we take:** every player has a bag of white (safe) and black (doom) tokens that everyone can see, and the race ends with a draw from your own bag. White, you win everything; black, everyone loses.
- **Where it goes:** every country starts with a bag of 2 white and 6 black. Develop cards that race ahead put doom tokens in your own bag (building data centres +1, accelerating towards AGI +3, a space data centre +1, AI military integration +1). Reinforce cards put safe tokens in (regulation +1, a government agency +1, interpretability +2, the killswitch +2). The bag shows beside each country's bars, with its chance of a safe draw.
- **What it changes:** in the CNA game, reaching AGI with low safety triggers a roll you can't see. Now the danger is a pile of tokens on the table that you built yourself, card by card, and everyone can count it. The argument gets sharper: the race doesn't end when you get there, it ends when you find out what you built (Bostrom's idea card).

### 2. Switching it on is a choice

- **What we take:** in *The Race*, entering the centre is a decision you pay for, not something that just happens.
- **Where it goes:** when your AI reaches 100, a new card appears in your hand, **Switch on superintelligence**. Play it and you draw from your bag. Or don't: wait a year, fill your bag with safe tokens, and hope nobody beats you to it.
- **What it changes:** it adds Press Your Luck. Being first and being safe pull against each other at the moment that matters most.

### 3. The three rings, laid along the AI bar

- **What we take:** *The Race*'s board has three rings (chatbots, agents, automated research) and stepping inward adds doom tokens.
- **Where it goes:** the AI bar is split into the same stages: chatbots below 40, agents from 40, automated research from 70, superintelligence at 100. Crossing into agents adds 1 doom token to your bag; crossing into automated research adds 2.
- **What it changes:** progress itself is risky, not just the cards that cause it. Every country that gets far carries some doom, however careful it has been.

### 4. The doom track becomes the world's AI risk

- **What we take:** in *The Race*, every black token added to any bag moves the doom track up, and at 12 the AI takes over and everyone loses.
- **Where it goes:** the CNA game already has a world AI risk meter. Now every doom token added to any bag raises it by 2. At 100 the AI takes over and everyone loses.
- **What it changes:** one country's shortcut raises the risk for everyone (Perrow's tightly coupled systems).

### 5. The AI as a piece on the board

- **What we take:** *The Race*'s AI pawn rolls a die every round, moves round the board, and sets off incidents where it stops. It moves further in as the doom track rises.
- **Where it goes:** at the end of every year the AI (🤖 on the map) rolls and stops in one of the countries still in the race, and that country draws an incident card. At world risk 40 it visits two countries a year; at 70, three.
- **What it changes:** the AI becomes a character that acts on its own (Non-Player Characters, Roll / Spin and Move), not just a number that rises.

### 6. The incident deck

- **What we take:** *The Race*'s Incident deck, with each card tied to a record in the database: the AI offers a deal (take 10 AI and 2 doom tokens next year, or refuse), a model escapes the sandbox, a safety breakthrough, a data centre fire, a public scandal (a lawsuit), leaked weights (China takes some of your AI; the database's model weights leak), talent poached, and grid strain (the electricity grid).
- **Where it goes:** the AI draws them for the countries it visits; the deal waits in your hand next year as a choice.

### 7. The pause treaty

- **What we take:** *The Race*'s treaty: it starts only if everyone votes yes; while it holds nobody steps inward and safety is cheaper; anyone can break it by stepping inward anyway; three rounds of treaty is a shared win.
- **Where it goes:** a new reinforce card, **Call for a pause**. The next year opens with an open vote (👍 or 👎), and the pause starts only if every country still in the race votes yes. While it holds, nobody's AI grows, safe-token cards give one extra token, and the cards that grow AI **break the pause** openly, for 10 allies. If the pause holds three years, everyone still standing shares the win.
- **What it changes:** the CNA game's only cooperation was the shared loss. Now there is a shared win too, and the game argues that it is fragile: one country that won't vote yes, or one that breaks it, is enough to end it (Voting, Cooperative Play).

## New in this combined game

### Hidden compute and inspections

- **What it is:** a new develop card, **Build in secret**, puts 8 AI in a sealed envelope (on the board it shows only as ✉), and it joins your AI at the end of the year, or when a pause ends. A new attack card, **Inspect their data centres**, opens a rival's envelope. Anyone caught building in secret during a pause loses what they hid and 15 allies; an empty envelope costs the inspector 3 economy.
- **Where it comes from:** it joins the CNA game's covert cards (spies, the honey trap) to *The Race*'s treaty. *The Race* itself now has no hidden compute: breaking its treaty is done in the open.
- **What it changes:** the pause can be broken without anyone seeing, so trust and checking become part of the game (Bluffing). Whether to keep this, or to match *The Race* and make every break open, is a choice to test in play.

### Prompt injection

A new incident from the database's risks: your agents are hijacked, and you lose 10 cybersecurity.

### What we leave out, for now

*The Race*'s money (capital and buying data centres on the board), its five asymmetric players (two states and three labs, with their own powers), and its trading. The CNA game's countries already differ in their starting bars and political systems. The labs inside each country are a good next step if the game needs more.

## From the break-the-datacentre game (not in yet)

That game isn't in yet. What we would look for in it, and where it would go:

- **Data centres as places on the map.** If it names real data centres (the database has many, from Stargate Abilene to Data Center Alley), each country could own one or two as pins on the map: targets for the CNA game's attack and hack cards, and places the AI stops.
- **Ways a data centre breaks.** Power, water, cooling, chips, workers: the database's risks (a drought, an outage, the grid, a strike) could become a new kind of attack, sabotage, or a new incident.
- **What breaking one costs.** If it argues something about who gets hurt when infrastructure fails, that belongs in the reveal: the log of what happened each year.

We will fold these in once we can play it, and credit it here by name.

## What stays as it is in the CNA game

The map and its colours, the five bars and what knocks you out, the four kinds of cards and their colours, the order cards resolve in (reinforce, develop, wildcards, attacks), the pass-the-laptop turns for up to four people, every attack card and wildcard, and every ending in the CNA game except the hidden roll at AGI, which the bag replaces.

The CNA game's newest version added five things, and all five are in this game too:

- **Lethal autonomous weapons**, the strongest attack. Here it also puts two doom tokens in the attacker's bag, so the most dangerous weapon makes your own draw at the centre worse.
- **Face-down wildcards**: twelve real events from the database, six that help and six that hurt, picked blind.
- **The power ranking**: one number for how well each country is doing. It doesn't count the bag, so the country at the top of the ranking can be the one most likely to draw black. That gap is part of what the combined game argues.
- **The Daily Compute**, the yearly front page with its made-up cast. Here it also reports the AI's stops, the pause votes, inspections, and the draw at the centre.
- **The class screen** for the projector, at `/remix/cna/screen`. Here it also shows the bags, the AI on the map and the pause votes.
