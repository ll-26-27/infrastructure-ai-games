Charles Perrow, "Complexity, Coupling, and Catastrophe," in *Normal Accidents: Living with High-Risk Technologies* (Princeton University Press, 1999), pp. 62–100.

Perrow argues that in some systems accidents are normal, built into the design. When a system is complex (its parts interact in unexpected ways) and tightly coupled (one failure spreads straight to the next, with no slack), small failures can combine into a catastrophe no operator could foresee. Nuclear power plants were his main example.
