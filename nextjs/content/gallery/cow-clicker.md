You have a cow. You may click it once every six hours, and each click earns you a "click." You can pay to click sooner, or buy fancier cows. When you click, the game posts to your friends' feeds about it. That is the whole game.

Ian Bogost, a game designer and scholar, made it in 2010 as a satire: "Cow Clicker is Facebook games distilled to their essence." It had more than 50,000 players within a few months, many of them playing it for real. In 2011 he ended it with the "Cowpocalypse," and the cows disappeared. Some players kept clicking where the cows used to be.

**Look for:** what's left when you strip a game down to its hooks: a timer, a payment, and your friends as an audience.
