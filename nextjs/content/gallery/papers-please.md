You are an immigration inspector in Arstotzka, a made-up Eastern Bloc country, in 1982. Each day a line of people comes to your booth, and you compare their passports, permits and papers against a rulebook that grows longer every morning. You are paid by the person processed, fined for mistakes, and each night you decide whether your pay goes to rent, food, heat or medicine for your family.

**Look for:** the rulebook as infrastructure. Every new rule is another check, another category a person can fail, and the speed the job demands makes the categories matter more than the people.
