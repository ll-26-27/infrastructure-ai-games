Players move around a square board, buy land, and collect rent from whoever lands on it. The owners get richer, everyone else gets poorer, and the board fills up until one player holds nearly everything. Magie designed it on purpose to teach that lesson: she called it "a practical demonstration of the present system of land-grabbing with all its usual outcomes and consequences."

People made their own boards and passed the game around for decades. One of those homemade versions became Monopoly, which Parker Brothers published in 1935; they also bought Magie's patent.

**Look for:** a game written as an argument. The rules make a claim about property, and you feel the claim by playing.
