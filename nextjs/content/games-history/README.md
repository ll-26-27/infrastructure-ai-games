The game you design today won't come from nowhere. Its mechanics will be borrowed from games that borrowed them in turn: a hidden map from an 1824 Prussian training game, a tech tree from Civilization, a crop that withers if you don't come back from FarmVille, a number that grows by itself from Cookie Clicker.

Below are five lines of games that matter for a game about AI and infrastructure. A line isn't a family tree, only influences that makers and critics have named. For each line the question is the one from [reading the mechanics](/reading-mechanics) (and the card on [game mechanics](/mechanics)): **what does it make you do, and what does doing it argue?** Ian Bogost calls this argument made through rules [*procedural rhetoric*](/cards/bogost) (see Paperclips).

The [gallery](/gallery) has a page for most of these games, and the [mechanics](/mechanics) pages explain the moves they share. This page puts them in order.
