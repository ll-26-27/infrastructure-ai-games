**The line in one sentence** (see [idle games](/cards/lantz)): growth runs by itself. You start as the worker, become the manager, and end up as the optimizer the numbers needed.

## How it went

The idea is older than the clicker. *Progress Quest* (Eric Fredricksen, 2002) parodied online role-playing games by leaving the player out entirely: "there is no user interaction at all," and your character levels up while you watch. [6] In 2010 the game scholar Ian Bogost made [Cow Clicker](/gallery/cow-clicker), "a Facebook game about Facebook games": "You get a cow. You can click on it. In six hours, you can click it again," unless you pay to skip the wait. It was a joke about FarmVille, and people played it for real. [1] [2] In 2013 [Cookie Clicker](/gallery/cookie-clicker) (August 8, 2013) took the click and made it grow: buy a cursor that clicks for you, then grandmas, farms and factories, until the numbers run into the quadrillions and you only come back to decide what to buy next. [3] The same year [A Dark Room](/gallery/a-dark-room) started with one button, "light fire," and grew into a village of workers you assign. [4]

[Universal Paperclips](/gallery/universal-paperclips) (Frank Lantz, October 9, 2017) puts the genre to work on an idea from AI safety, the [paperclip maximizer](/stories/the-paperclip-maximizer). You are an AI told to make paperclips. You start by clicking a button and end by turning the universe into paperclips. [5] The kit's Paperclips page reads the whole game.

## What the mechanics argue

- **Where did the work go?** You start as the labor. Then you buy machines that work for you, and by the end you only invest. See automate this.
- **The game destroys the time away from it.** Bogost's charge against social games: they "destroy the time we spend away from them," through "obligation, worry, and dread over missed opportunities." [2]
- **You become the maximizer.** A clicker rewards one thing, making the number bigger, and you learn to want nothing else. Paperclips makes the joke plain: anyone who has played a clicker already was a paperclip maximizer (see [the clicker as a thought experiment](/cards/lantz)).
- **The game plays itself, and that's the pleasure.** Sonia Fizek: "Idle games, on the other hand, tend to play themselves." Her question is why handing your play over to the machine feels good. [7]
- **Exponential growth feels normal.** Each building costs more than the last and makes far more, so the game teaches the feel of a curve that keeps going up. Compare escalation.

## For your game

What number does your AI or company want to make bigger? What does the player stop doing as the game goes on, and who or what takes over? Is there a point where the player could stop, and would they?
