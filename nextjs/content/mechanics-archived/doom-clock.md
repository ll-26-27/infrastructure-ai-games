**What it is.** Two bars on the screen: yours, and the one that ends the game. You can slow the second bar, sometimes push it back, but it never stops for long. Every decision is weighed against the time it costs.

## How it plays

- **[Plague Inc.](../gallery/plague-inc.md)** (2012). Once the world notices your disease, scientists start on a cure, and a cure bar begins to fill. Your evolution has to outrun it. [1]
- **XCOM 2** (Firaxis, 2016). The aliens' "Avatar Project" fills a bar of pips. When it is full, a countdown starts, and if it runs out, you lose. Destroying their facilities knocks pips off. [2]
- **[Frostpunk](../gallery/frostpunk.md)** (2018). In the main scenario, word comes that a great storm is on its way, and the city has to be ready when it hits. [3]
- **[Pandemic](../gallery/pandemic.md)** (2008). The players also lose if the player deck runs out before they find the cures, so the deck is a clock too. [4]

## What it argues

The doom clock makes time the enemy. It also sets the pace: nothing can be done slowly, carefully or by consensus if the bar is about to fill. Doom-clock games reward the player who treats every turn as an emergency, and they make emergency feel like the natural condition.

Your Oct 26 readings treat time as something infrastructure makes, not just measures. Eva-Maria Nyckel shows Amazon's "anticipatory shipping" moving goods toward you before you order them. Steven Gonzalez Monserrate looks at data centers judged by uptime, where a few minutes of downtime is a crisis. A doom clock is the same idea in a game: a system that sets the tempo of everyone inside it. Ask who builds the clock, and who benefits from everyone believing it can't be stopped.

## Two ways in

**From a game you know.** Battle royale circles, survival games' nights, *Majora's Mask*'s falling moon, a ranked season's end date. Pick one. Does the clock make the game better, or just faster?

**Through its lineage.** The *Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists* has kept a Doomsday Clock since 1947, moving its hands closer to or further from midnight as the danger changes. [5] Games borrowed the image, and the habit of treating danger as minutes left.
