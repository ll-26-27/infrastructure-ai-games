**What it is.** Building something costs once. Keeping it running costs every turn: fuel, repairs, staff, maintenance. The more you build, the more you owe, until upkeep eats the growth.

## How it plays

- **[Frostpunk](../gallery/frostpunk.md)** (2018). The city lives around a coal-fired generator. It heats everything near it, and it has to be fed coal constantly. Turning it up keeps people alive in a cold snap and burns your stockpile faster. [1]
- **[SimCity](../gallery/simcity.md)** (1989 onward). In the 1989 game, roads, rails, and police and fire stations all have yearly costs paid out of taxes. A city can grow faster than its budget, and if you underfund transportation, roads and rails fall apart. [2]
- **RollerCoaster Tycoon** (Chris Sawyer, 1999). Rides break down, sometimes with guests on board, unless you hire mechanics to inspect and fix them. [3]

## What it argues

Growth-and-upkeep games refuse the idea that infrastructure, once built, simply exists. They put the maintenance on the screen as a running cost. The boring work of keeping things going becomes the game's main pressure.

Susan Leigh Star (Sept 21) describes infrastructure as invisible until it fails: "The normally invisible quality of working infrastructure becomes visible when it breaks: the server is down, the bridge washes out, there is a power blackout." She also writes that infrastructure "is fixed in modular increments, not all at once or globally." Upkeep games make the invisible part visible all the time. Steven Gonzalez Monserrate (Oct 26) looks at what it takes to keep a data center cool and running: the upkeep of the cloud is heat, water and power. Who pays the upkeep in a system you use?

## Two ways in

**From a game you know.** Tools that break in *Zelda: Breath of the Wild*, durability in survival games, pets you must feed, a guild hall's rent. Pick one. Does the upkeep make the game more fun or less? Why did the designers want it?

**Through its lineage.** City-building games have charged running costs since *SimCity*. [2] *Tamagotchi* (1996) made a whole toy out of upkeep: a creature that dies if you don't feed and clean it. [4]
