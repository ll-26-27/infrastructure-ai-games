**What it is.** The map is a network: cities joined by flights, regions joined by borders, buildings joined by streets. Something starts at one node and moves along the links. Each turn it reaches more, and the question is whether it covers everything before it can be stopped.

## How it plays

- **[Plague Inc.](../gallery/plague-inc.md)** (Ndemic Creations, 2012). You *are* the disease. You choose where to start, then spend "DNA points" to evolve ways of spreading (by air, water, insects) and symptoms. Ports and airports carry you between countries, and they close as the world notices. You win by infecting and killing everyone before humanity finishes a cure. [1]
- **[Pandemic](../gallery/pandemic.md)** (Matt Leacock, Z-Man Games, 2008). The same story from the other side: a cooperative board game in which two to four players, as a medic, a scientist and other specialists, fly between connected cities to treat disease and find cures. Each turn, infection cards add disease cubes to cities, and the infection spreads along the lines on the board. [2]
- **[They Are Billions](../gallery/they-are-billions.md)** (Numantian Games, 2017 in early access, 2019 in full). You build a colony while hordes of the infected wander outside. If they get in, the people they reach become infected too, and one breach can turn a whole district. [3]

## What it argues

*Plague Inc.* and *Pandemic* use the same mechanic and ask you to stand on opposite sides of it. In one you want the network to be as connected as possible; in the other, every connection is a liability. Ports, flights and borders, the infrastructure that makes the world work, are exactly what the spread runs on.

That links to Susan Leigh Star (Sept 21): infrastructure has "reach or scope … beyond a single event or one-site practice." Reach is what makes it useful and what makes it a route for the next contagion. Charles Perrow (Nov 16) argues that the more tightly coupled a system is, the faster a local failure becomes everyone's. A spread game is a lesson in coupling: you learn to read a map by where things can travel.

## Two ways in

**From a game you know.** Spread is everywhere: zombies in survival games, the hidden traitor's influence in *Among Us*, territory painting in *Splatoon*, a rumor in a social game. Pick one. What travels, and along what links?

**Through its lineage.** Epidemiologists have modeled spread since Kermack and McKendrick's equations of 1927, which sort a population into susceptible, infected and recovered. [4] *Plague Inc.*'s designer has said he was inspired by the Flash game *Pandemic 2* (2008) and the film *Contagion* (2011). [1] Leacock's *Pandemic* came out in 2008 too, and its maker has spoken about designing it as a cooperative game. [2]

**In a story:** [Ring](../stories/ring.md) and [Invasion of the Body Snatchers](../stories/invasion-of-the-body-snatchers.md) are both spread stories.
