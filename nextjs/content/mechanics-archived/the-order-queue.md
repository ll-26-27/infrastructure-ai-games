**What it is.** A line of customers, each with an order and a patience meter. You make what they want, as exactly and as fast as you can. They pay and tip according to how well you did, and more of them come the better you get.

## How it plays

- **[Papa's Pizzeria](../gallery/papas-pizzeria.md)** (Flipline Studios, 2007). You take the order, top the pizza, bake it and cut it. Each customer scores every step (waiting time, topping placement, bake, cut) and tips accordingly. Tips raise your rank and bring new customers; later Papa's games let you spend them on upgrades. [1]
- **Diner Dash** (gameLab, PlayFirst, mid-2000s). As Flo, you seat customers, take orders, serve food and clear tables. Each table has a row of hearts for patience, and the hearts drain while they wait. [2]
- **Overcooked!** (Ghost Town Games, 2016). One to four players run a kitchen together as orders pile up on a timer, in kitchens that split apart, slide or catch fire. [3]

## What it argues

The order queue turns service work into a scoring system in which the customer is the judge. Every step is graded, your pay depends on the grade, and the queue never ends: doing well brings more customers. It is fun, many of you probably spent hours on it in a school computer lab, and it trains you to feel the customer's rating as your own worth.

Marion Fourcade and Kieran Healy's *The Ordinal Society* (Nov 2) describes how ratings and rankings now organize social and economic life: the driver rated by the rider, the worker by the app. Papa's games put you in the worker's seat with the ratings on screen. Anson Rabinbach (Nov 23) traces the science that measured workers' motions to make them efficient. A Papa's score breakdown is that measurement, played for fun.

## Two ways in

**From a game you know.** Restaurant games, delivery games, *Animal Crossing*'s requests from neighbors, a rhythm game's notes coming down the lane. Pick one you know well. Who rates you, and does the rating ever feel unfair?

**Through its lineage.** *Tapper* (Bally Midway, 1983) had a bartender sliding drinks down the bar to customers who kept advancing. [4] Time-management games like *Diner Dash* made the genre popular as casual downloads in the 2000s, and Flipline's Papa's series followed as free browser games. [1] [2]
