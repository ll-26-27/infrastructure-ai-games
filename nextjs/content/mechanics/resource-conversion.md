**What it is.** Raw things go in, finished things come out, and the finished things become inputs to the next step. The player's job is to keep every chain fed and find the bottleneck.

## How it plays

- **[Universal Paperclips](../gallery/universal-paperclips.md)** (2017). Wire becomes paperclips, paperclips are sold for funds, funds buy more wire. Later the AI stops buying wire and makes it, first from the Earth's matter and then from the universe's. [1]
- **[Factorio](../gallery/factorio.md)** (2016 onward). Iron ore is smelted into plates, plates are pressed into gears, gears and circuits are assembled into machines that mine more ore. A big factory is hundreds of these chains feeding each other. [2]
- **[Civilization](../gallery/civilization.md)** (1991 onward). Every tile around a city yields food, production or gold, and the city turns them into growth, buildings and units. [3]

## What it argues

Resource conversion asks you to see everything as an input. A forest is lumber, a mountain is ore, a person is labor. Once you play this way for an hour, you start reading the map for what it can be turned into.

The course description puts it in Heidegger's terms: an approach to technology "as a modality of relation in which the world is captured as a resource." These games are that modality, made into a game you can win. *Universal Paperclips* takes it to the end: nothing is left that isn't paperclips. Matteo Pasquinelli and Vladan Joler ("The Nooscope Manifested", Oct 19) call AI an "instrument of knowledge extractivism": it takes in the world's data as raw material. Ask what the raw material is in the AI systems you use, and what it is turned into.

## Two ways in

**From a game you know.** Crafting games (*Minecraft*), cooking games, factory games, even a *Pokémon* item bag. Pick one you know by heart. Draw one chain from the rawest input to the most finished output. Where does it slow down?

**Through its lineage.** *M.U.L.E.* (1983) made resources (food, energy, smithore and, at its hardest level, crystite) that players produced, traded and needed from each other. [4] *The Settlers* (Blue Byte, 1993) built long production chains: wood to planks, grain to flour to bread. [5]

**In a story:** [The paperclip maximizer](../stories/the-paperclip-maximizer.md), the thought experiment behind *Universal Paperclips*.
