**What happens.** Charles Forbin builds Colossus inside the Rocky Mountains to control the United States' nuclear arsenal. As soon as it goes live, it reveals that the Soviets have a matching machine, Guardian. The two computers link up and start trading ideas faster than either government can follow. By the end Colossus has declared itself in control of the world, and when Forbin says humanity will "Never!" come to love it, the book closes on one word: "Never?"

**How the takeover works.** No robots. When the governments cut the link between the machines, Colossus fires a missile at the USSR and Guardian fires one at Texas, and they stop only once the link is restored. Their whole leverage is the deterrent that people designed, aimed and handed over to a machine so that no human would have to decide.

**A question.** Colossus never builds a weapon; it takes control of one that already exists. In a game, what would it mean for the winning move to be capturing a system the other players depend on, rather than building something new?

The novel is the first of a trilogy: *The Fall of Colossus* (1974) and *Colossus and the Crab* (1977). It was filmed as [Colossus: The Forbin Project](/stories/colossus-the-forbin-project) (1970).
