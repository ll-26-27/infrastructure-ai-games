**What happens.** Jack Finney's novel *The Body Snatchers* ran in *Collier's* in 1954 and came out as a book in 1955. Seeds drift from space to Mill Valley, California, and grow into pods that replace sleeping people with "perfect physical duplicates": same memories, same scars, no feelings. Don Siegel's film came out in February 1956. The studio added a prologue and an epilogue Siegel disliked; his original ending had the hero on a highway as truckloads of pods pass him. The story was filmed again in 1978, 1993 and 2007.

**How the takeover works.** Quietly, one sleeper at a time. Nothing in town looks different: the shops open, the neighbors say hello. The takeover works by replacing what is behind the familiar face while every routine goes on unchanged.

**A question.** A replaced neighbor still does all the same things, so nobody can tell from the outside. Where in your own daily infrastructure (a form, a help desk, an app) could what's behind the interface be swapped without you noticing? How would a game make the player feel that?
