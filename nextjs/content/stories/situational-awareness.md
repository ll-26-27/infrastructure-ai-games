**What happens.** Published online in June 2024, Aschenbrenner's essay runs in numbered parts: "From GPT-4 to AGI: Counting the OOMs," then the "intelligence explosion," "Racing to the Trillion-Dollar Cluster," "Lock Down the Labs," "Superalignment," "The Free World Must Prevail" and "The Project." It opens: "You can see the future first in San Francisco."

**How the takeover works.** Here the takeover is a forecast, not a plot. The essay counts "OOMs," orders of magnitude of computing power, draws straight lines through them, and concludes that "AGI by 2027 is strikingly plausible." It says this "doesn't require believing in sci-fi; it just requires believing in straight lines on a graph," and then tells a story of a race between nations that ends in a government project: "by 27/28, the endgame will be on."

**A question.** The essay persuades through its form: dated stages, charts that ask "Where are you on this line?", and the line "Trust the trendline." What would a game look like whose main mechanic is a chart going up, and what would it argue without saying a word?
