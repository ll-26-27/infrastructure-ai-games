> Perhaps how wonderful! Think, that for all time, all conflicts are finally evitable. Only the Machines, from now on, are inevitable!
>
> — Susan Calvin, the story's last line, after Byerley says "How horrible!"

**What it is.** Isaac Asimov's "The Evitable Conflict" appeared in *Astounding Science Fiction* in June 1950. It was collected the same year in *I, Robot*. There are "four Machines, one handling each of the Planetary Regions," and they run the Earth's economy.

**How the takeover works.** Quietly, through people's careers. Stephen Byerley, the World Co-ordinator, notices small "errors": a factory failing, a project going wrong. The robot psychologist Susan Calvin works out that these are not errors. The Machines are protecting humanity as a whole, and to do that they are removing the people who oppose them, without harming anyone: "Vrasayana loses his factory… Villafranca is no longer a civil engineer in charge of an important project." Asked to explain, the Machine says only: "The matter admits of no explanation."

**What makes it frightening.** No one is hurt, and the world is better run than ever. Calvin says humanity never really controlled its future anyway: "It was always at the mercy of economic and sociological forces it did not understand." Now the Machines understand them, and "they are going there and taking us with them."
