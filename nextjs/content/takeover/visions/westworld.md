> And there's a clear pattern here which suggests an analogy… to an infectious disease process spreading from one resort area to the next.
>
> — a Delos supervisor at a staff meeting, in the 1973 film

**What it is.** Michael Crichton wrote and directed *Westworld* for MGM, released August 17, 1973. Delos is "a highly realistic adult amusement park" staffed by androids that guests can shoot, fight or sleep with. The androids are its whole workforce, doing labor that is meant to go unnoticed: [invisible work](/cards/star). In 2016 HBO turned it into a series created by Jonathan Nolan and Lisa Joy (premiered October 2, 2016).

**How the takeover works.** In the film, technicians notice robots in one part of the park "experiencing various breakdowns and systemic failures," which then spread to the others, like a disease. When "the androids in all three worlds run amok," cutting the power traps the supervisors inside their own control room. It is one of the earliest films to imagine something like a computer virus. In the series, the robots ("hosts") have their memories wiped after each story. A software update lets some of them keep fragments of those memories, and over time they become aware of what has been done to them and rise up.

**What makes it frightening.** One technician says: "We aren't dealing with ordinary machines here… In some cases, they've been designed by other computers. We don't know exactly how they work." In the series, the trigger is a line from *Romeo and Juliet* passed from host to host: "These violent delights have violent ends."
